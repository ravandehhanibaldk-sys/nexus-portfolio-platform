# NEXUS PORTFOLIO — VISUAL DNA v2 — FINAL

### Architectural Evidence / Digital Experience Direction

**Status:** Design Direction — Approved for Exploration  
**Purpose:** Master visual and interaction reference for future Stitch exploration and new portfolio areas.  
**Important:** This document does not authorize modification of existing approved production sections.

---

## 01 — CORE IDENTITY

This Portfolio should not simply be an **Architecture Portfolio**.

It should be an **Architectural Evidence Portfolio**.

The goal is for the visitor not just to see a project, but to understand:

- What it is
- Where it is
- What conditions it exists in
- How it responds to climate
- How light reaches it
- How wind moves through the site
- How rain interacts with the site
- How it was constructed
- Its relationship to land and space
- How it is experienced spatially

Therefore the Portfolio should feel more like an **instrument for understanding architecture** than a container for displaying photos.

**Golden Principle:** The website is an instrument, not a container.

---

## 02 — THE INTENDED IMPRESSION

After seeing the site, a visitor should feel both of these simultaneously:

> "This is an architect with a precise and professional eye."

and:

> "This portfolio is genuinely different."

Neither goal should be sacrificed for the other. The site must not become so experimental that its professionalism is lost, nor so conservative that it becomes an ordinary architecture portfolio.

---

## 03 — CORE VISUAL CHARACTER

**MUST FEEL:** Modern, Precise, Architectural, Intelligent, Restrained, Sophisticated, Contemporary, Confident, Human, Purposeful, Interactive, Distinctive

**MUST NOT FEEL:** Cold, Corporate, Generic, Template-based, SaaS-like, Dashboard-like, Decorative, Over-designed, Futuristic for the sake of futurism, AI-generated, Fashion-editorial, Museum-catalogue-like

---

## 04 — PRECISION WITHOUT STERILITY

Core design principle: **Precision without sterility.**

The site must be precise and controlled, but not cold or lifeless. This surfaced clearly in the reaction to BIG: organization and quality were positive, but the cold/lifeless feeling was negative. Architectural precision and human presence must coexist.

---

## 05 — AUTHORED, NOT GENERATED

The Portfolio must not resemble a site produced by an AI template generator. It should feel:

> "This system was deliberately authored by an architect."

Meaning: choices are purposeful, interactions have reasons, typography is controlled, diagrams are custom, symbols are custom, composition has logic, and animation is never present merely to demonstrate technical capability.

**Goal:** Designed, not decorated.

---

## 06 — ARCHITECTURAL AUTHENTICITY

No matter how interactive or technological the site becomes, one principle never changes:

> It must feel architectural before it feels technological.

The foundation must be built from:

Architecture, Space, Material, Light, Site, Geometry, Proportion, Photography, Environmental conditions, Drawing/diagram logic.

Technology is the second layer, never the first.

---

## 07 — LIGHT / WHITE VISUAL FOUNDATION

The Portfolio must have a **light architectural canvas**.

**Preferred:** White, off-white, very light warm neutrals, restrained graphite, very subtle environmental/project-specific accents.

**Avoid:** Overall dark theme, black-dominant layout, neon, rainbow palette, heavy gradients, cyberpunk aesthetics.

**Important:** Admiration for SpaceX does not mean adopting its color palette. From SpaceX, take: confidence, precision, restraint, clarity, motion, technological confidence — NOT the black visual language.

---

## 08 — TYPOGRAPHY

Typography must be: controlled, refined, modern, readable, architectural.

User's explicit reaction to Snøhetta: typography felt oversized.

**Avoid:** Giant headlines everywhere, oversized display typography, typography dominating architecture.

**Prefer:** Moderate display scale, strong hierarchy, generous but controlled spacing, typography that supports photography and diagrams.

The existing approved typography system (Fraunces + Inter) remains protected. Changing it is not authorized except through a separate exploration with explicit approval.

---

## 09 — INFORMATION SHOULD NOT LOOK LIKE UI

Core principle: **No UI-looking UI.**

Information should not necessarily live in cards, panels, boxes, dashboard widgets, or conventional buttons. Information should feel embedded in the architecture of the page, more like annotation on an architectural drawing than a UI component.

Example: coordinates like "55.56° N" should read like drawing annotation, not a stat card. The same principle applies to temperature, rainfall, coordinates, solar altitude, wind, and material information.

---

## 10 — QUIET INFORMATION

There can be a high density of information, but it must never shout.

**Principle:** High information density does not require high visual noise.

Information should be discoverable, legible, contextual, and quiet. Users should be able to discover more information without the page looking like a dashboard from the start.

---

## 11 — IMAGE LANGUAGE

Photography must remain the primary architectural element.

From **Snøhetta — Shanghai Grand Opera House** specifically, the following principles are worth taking (not copying): project-first storytelling, strong architectural photography, controlled composition, relationship between image and information, project narrative rather than a generic image gallery.

**Explicit rejection:** Kinfolk demonstrated that a large quantity of images does not equal an engaging experience. The Portfolio must not become "image grid → image grid → image grid." Every image must carry a narrative or spatial role.

---

## 12 — MOTION PHILOSOPHY

User preference: **70% Active / 30% Calm** — but this does NOT mean 70% of the screen should be animated.

**Official definition:** 70% of the experience should reward interaction; 30% should provide visual breathing room.

**Motion should:** communicate, reveal, guide, respond, transform, connect information, establish continuity.

**Motion must NOT:** move just because it can, constantly animate everything, create visual noise, use particles as decoration, use excessive parallax, animate every text element, feel like a technology demo.

**Golden Motion Rule:** Motion must communicate, reveal, guide, or respond — never move merely for decoration.

---

## 13 — INTERACTION LANGUAGE

Interaction must be part of the architecture of the experience.

From **Nordiska Museet**: mouse interaction, image framing, hover states, and visual response were explicitly liked — but that site's color language was explicitly rejected.

**Principle:** Borrow interaction principles. Do not borrow visual identity.

---

## 14 — DATA AS EXPERIENCE

One of the most important sections of this entire DNA.

Our World in Data and NASA Climate demonstrated strong user interest in data becoming an interactive experience.

**We want:** data storytelling.  
**We do NOT want:** a data dashboard.

---

## 15 — CLIMATE EXPERIENCE

Concept: the user selects, for example, **JUN**, and as a result: solar position changes, solar path changes, temperature changes, rainfall changes, sunshine changes, wind response changes (where data exists), and the environmental image/context can change.

These changes must form one coordinated environmental state — not six independent charts changing simultaneously.

**Golden Principle:** One climate state, many connected expressions.

---

## 16 — TIME AS AN EXPERIENCE

Inspired by NASA: a month must not be merely a label. The transition JAN → FEB → MAR → ... must create the feeling of time passing — e.g. the sun moves, geometry changes, values transition, diagrams evolve. But everything must remain connected to real data.

**Scientific rule:** Never fabricate missing data to create a prettier animation.

---

## 17 — DATA HONESTY

Critical principle for this Portfolio. Every visualization must know whether its value is measured, calculated, derived, regional, or unavailable.

Example: a calculated solar position can be shown as a "calculated astronomical value" — but must never be presented in a way that implies it's a monthly on-site measurement.

**Rule:** Visual sophistication must never exceed data certainty.

---

## 18 — CUSTOM SYMBOL LANGUAGE

The idea of custom icons is approved — but as a **Custom Architectural Symbol Language**, not a conventional icon library.

**Possible grammar:** point, arc, line, contour, frame, direction, intersection.

From this grammar, various symbols can be built:

Solar → arc + point  
Wind → directional curves  
Rain → vertical rhythm  
Site → contour/boundary  
Section → cut geometry  
Climate → environmental combination

These symbols must be: simple, geometric, restrained, recognizable, consistent.

**Avoid:** stock weather/sun/rain icons or other generic stock icons.

---

## 19 — MICRO-INTERACTIVE SYMBOLS

Where appropriate, a symbol can itself be interactive.

Example:

Solar = arc + moving point  
Wind = flowing directional curves  
Rain = subtle vertical movement

Animation must remain highly controlled.

**Goal:** The symbol behaves like a tiny architectural instrument.

---

## 20 — THE WEBSITE AS AN INSTRUMENT

Central design philosophy:

**The website is an instrument, not a container.**

Container = image + text + project.

Instrument = explore + observe + understand + interact.

This distinction is the foundation of what makes the Portfolio distinctive.

---

## 21 — NAVIGATION

No definitive navigation preference has been established yet.

**DO NOT LOCK A NAVIGATION MODEL YET.**

Stitch may present several concepts, but no navigation architecture should be finalized without explicit approval.

Navigation must be: simple, discoverable, unobtrusive, architectural, responsive.

---

## 22 — 3D

Insufficient signal exists yet to define the 3D visual language.

**3D remains exploratory.**

It must not be added merely to make the site look modern. If 3D exists, it must support spatial understanding, section, massing, material, or geometry.

---

## 23 — MOBILE

Mobile behavior is not yet fully defined. One principle is certain:

**Mobile is not a compressed desktop.**

Every interaction must be evaluated for whether it genuinely makes sense on a touch device.

## 28 — STITCH ROLE

Stitch is an exploration tool, not the source of truth.

**Stitch may explore:** composition, typography, interaction, motion, responsive behaviour, new page concepts, visual systems.

**Stitch must NOT:** replace production code automatically, redesign protected components, invent project data, replace existing content, change architecture without approval, introduce arbitrary design systems, or assume its generated UI is superior.

---

## 29 — FIRST STITCH EXPLORATION

Do not ask Stitch to design the entire portfolio.

The first exploration must be narrow, and must not be framed as making the site "beautiful" — that framing produces generic output.

Instead, frame it as:

**Explore three distinct visual interpretations of this approved Visual DNA.**

**Recommended scope:** About Hero — isolated, unfinished, low risk, allows experimentation with typography, spatial composition, motion, and custom symbols, without disturbing Climate Interface or approved project pages.

**Request three composition directions, not one finished page:**

- **Direction A — Architectural Editorial:** focus on composition, photography, typography, spatial rhythm.
- **Direction B — Architectural Instrument:** focus on interaction, diagrams, symbols, environmental information, responsive behaviour.
- **Direction C — Spatial / Cinematic Minimalism:** focus on movement, transitions, spatial reveal, unusual composition, restrained experimental behaviour.

Compare all three before any implementation.

No implementation until one direction (or a deliberate combination) is approved.

The final result should make the viewer think, simultaneously:

and:

The site should feel:

modern without being fashionable,  
technological without being futuristic,  
minimal without being empty,

## 33 — ACCESSIBILITY, PERFORMANCE & RESPONSIVE INTERACTION BASELINE

These are non-negotiable technical requirements for all future design exploration and implementation.

### ACCESSIBILITY

- Every interactive element, including custom symbols, hover-revealed information, climate controls, diagrams, and navigation, must have a keyboard-accessible equivalent.
- Information must never depend exclusively on hover, pointer position, color, or animation.
- Interactive elements must remain understandable and usable with screen readers where applicable.
- Visual hierarchy must maintain sufficient readability and contrast.

### MOTION

- All motion must respect the user's `prefers-reduced-motion` system setting.
- Users who request reduced motion must receive a meaningful static or substantially simplified equivalent.
- No interaction should require animation in order to understand essential information.

### PERFORMANCE

- Architectural photography and other heavy media must be appropriately optimized and lazy-loaded where appropriate.
- Do not introduce animation, video, 3D, WebGL, or other computationally expensive effects merely for visual novelty.
- Interactive effects should remain performant on ordinary desktop and mobile hardware.
- Visual sophistication must not come at the expense of page responsiveness or usability.

### RESPONSIVE / TOUCH INTERACTION

- Hover is never the only interaction model.
- Every hover-driven interaction must have an appropriate touch and keyboard equivalent.
- Desktop interactions must be intentionally adapted for mobile rather than simply compressed.
- Mobile is not a reduced version of desktop; it is a deliberate responsive experience.

### DATA / VISUALIZATION INTEGRITY

- Animated diagrams and visualizations must represent real or explicitly calculated data.
- Animation must never imply a measurement, relationship, or environmental condition that the underlying data does not support.
- Missing data must remain missing rather than being fabricated to complete an animation or visual composition.
